# Job search evidence memo

Date: 2026-11-11. Contract h-20261111-0837ba.

## Bottom line (confidence: medium on direction, low on most effect sizes)

- Referral and sourced channels convert far better than cold inbound applications. Direction is consistent across sources. Magnitudes depend on one vendor dataset (Ashby) and are not causal.
- Few rigorous causal studies exist for resume tailoring, cover letters, follow-ups, Easy Apply vs company site, informational interviews, or career fairs. Most published numbers are vendor or blog figures (grade C/D).
- The entry-level market is soft but not collapsing. Recent-grad unemployment was 5.2% in 2026:Q3 (NY Fed). Aggregate hires rate was 3.4% in September 2026 (BLS JOLTS).
- The "80% hidden job market" figure has no traceable primary source. Treat it as a myth in size, plausible in direction.
- Salary negotiation has the best-quantified payoff of any lever here, from a large, pre-registered-style academic experiment (still a working paper or brief, grade B).

## Method and limits

Every source below was reached through web search or fetch on 2026-11-11. I fetched only the NY Fed page, the BLS JOLTS release and the Ashby referrals page directly. Everything else is from search-result summaries and is marked "secondary" where I did not open the original. Search summaries sometimes disagree with each other; conflicts are flagged, not resolved.

Grades:
- A = official statistics or peer-reviewed.
- B = working paper, professional-association survey, or other reputable non-peer-reviewed research.
- C = vendor first-party data analysis (large sample, but self-interested, methods not independently reviewed).
- D = vendor marketing, blog, or unsourced secondary claim.

Labels: FACT = number read from the cited source. ESTIMATE = my inference or arithmetic, stated as such.

## 1. Funnel rates by channel

### 1a. Ashby ATS data, Jan 2021 to Dec 2024, 38M+ applications across 93K jobs (grade C, FACT, fetched)

Source: https://www.ashbyhq.com/talent-trends-report/reports/referrals (accessed 2026-11-11)

| Channel | Application-to-interview | Interview-to-offer |
|---|---|---|
| Internal | 42% | 32% |
| Agency | 42% | 8% |
| Referral | 40% | 16% |
| Sourced (recruiter outreach) | 25% | 6% |
| Inbound (cold apply) | 3% | 6% |

- Offer rate per application, quarterly averages Q3 2021 to Q4 2024: referral fell from 12.1% to 7.3%. Inbound fell from 0.7% to 0.2%. Same page.
- Referrals were about 1% of applications on average over 2021-2024. Same page. The page does not give referral share of hires.
- ESTIMATE: chaining the table gives application-to-offer of about 0.40 x 0.16 = 6.4% for referral and 0.03 x 0.06 = 0.18% for inbound, a ratio of about 35x. This is compounded from rounded figures, so treat as order of magnitude. It does not match the page's own quarterly offer-rate chart (7.3% vs 0.2%, about 36x at Q4 2024), which is reassuring on order of magnitude only.
- Caveat: Ashby customers skew toward tech and startups. "Referral" candidates are pre-selected by employees, so this is selection, not the causal effect of asking for a referral.

### 1b. Share of hires vs share of applicants

- Ashby: inbound reached 52% of all hires in Q2 2025; referrals about 17% of hires, steady regardless of inbound volume (nearly 250K hires, Jan 2021 to Jun 2025). Grade C, secondary via search summary. https://www.ashbyhq.com/talent-trends-report/reports/inbound
- Combining with 1a (ESTIMATE): referrals are about 1% of applications but about 17% of hires. That is roughly 17x over-representation. Inbound is the large majority of applications but 52% of hires. These two figures come from different time windows and reports, so the combination is rough.
- Conflict: one secondary write-up reports referrals as 6% of applicants, 21% of interviewees, 27% of offers, 29% of hires (about 4.8x). I could not confirm it against Ashby. Grade D. https://www.noon.ai/blog/articles/360-employee-referral-statistics-2026
- Applications per hire: Greenhouse 203 across 6,000+ North American employers in 2025 (secondary, grade C/D). https://www.herohunt.ai/blog/recruiting-funnel-conversion-rates-2026-stage-benchmarks/ . Another secondary source reports Ashby at 291 per hire in Q1 2026 (unconfirmed). https://www.ashbyhq.com/talent-trends-report/reports/startup-hiring
- Old data: Lever 2015-2016 study, "1 in 100 candidates is hired". Grade C, dated. https://www.shrm.org/topics-tools/news/talent-acquisition/lever-study-shows-1-100-candidates-hired

### 1c. Channels with little or no usable data

- Career fairs: NACE surveys show employers rate career fairs highly for effectiveness (4.9 out of 5 in the 2016 survey) and attend 38 to 48 a year (2017-18, 2018-19). That is employer perception and attendance, not conversion. Grade B. https://ebiztest.naceweb.org/talent-acquisition/branding-and-marketing/number-of-career-fairs-attended-holds-steady . I found no application-to-offer rate for career fairs.
- Intern and co-op sourcing, NACE 2017: career fairs used by 98.5% of employers and rated effective by 80.6%; intern referrals used by 92.6%, rated effective by 50.8%. Grade B, secondary via search summary. https://ebiztest.naceweb.org/talent-acquisition/internships/the-most-used-most-effective-methods-for-recruiting-interns-co-ops/
- Staffing agencies: Staffing Industry Analysts survey of 600+ firms puts median temp-to-perm conversion near 15%, rising above 40% after 3-4 consecutive assignments. Industry source with a commercial interest. Grade C/D. https://www.staffingindustry.com/editorial/staffing-industry-review/research-report-debunking-myth . A Boston Fed paper warns of secondary-labor-market traps. Grade B. https://www.bostonfed.org/-/media/Documents/cddp/cddp1603.pdf . Agency candidates in Ashby data convert to interview well (42%) but to offer poorly (8%), see 1a.
- Alumni networks: NOT FOUND. No conversion data located.
- Informational interviews: NOT FOUND. No outcome study located.
- LinkedIn Easy Apply vs company site: no controlled study found. Blog figures (3-13% vs 15-30% response; 12% vs 18% interview) are grade D and conflict with each other. https://www.autoapplymax.com/blog/linkedin-easy-apply-vs-external-application . One source itself concedes no controlled study exists. https://careery.pro/blog/job-boards-vs-company-website-which-is-better
- Peer-reviewed network evidence: LinkedIn randomized experiment on about 20 million users over five years (about 2 billion new ties, 600,000 new jobs) supports weak ties; the effect is an inverted U, with moderately weak ties best, and weak ties help more in digital industries. Grade A. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36107999/ and https://news.mit.edu/2022/weak-ties-linkedin-employment-0915

## 2. Time to hire and search length

- SHRM median time-to-fill 2025: 44 days non-executive, 45 executive (secondary, other pages say about 42). Grade B/C. https://www.noon.ai/blog/articles/181-time-to-hire-benchmarks-2026
- Gem: time-to-hire rose from 33 days (2021) to 41 days (2024), with interviews per hire up 42%. Grade C, secondary. https://vercel.gem.com/blog/10-takeaways-from-the-2025-recruiting-benchmarks-report
- Employ 2025: 46.2 days first interaction to accepted offer; 63.5 days posting to accepted offer (67.7 in 2024). Grade C. https://hrdive.com/news/hiring-benchmarks-report-employ-2025-more-applicants/809604
- Unemployed job seekers, BLS, March 2026 (not seasonally adjusted): median duration 12.8 weeks (10.9 a year earlier), mean 26.1 weeks. Grade A. https://www.bls.gov/web/empsit/cpseea35.htm
- Long-term unemployed (27+ weeks): 1.8 million, 25.4% of unemployed in March 2026, vs 21.3% a year earlier. Grade A. https://www.bls.gov/opub/ted/2026/short-term-unemployed-accounted-for-29-8-percent-of-total-unemployed-in-march-2026.htm
- 1,944 thousand unemployed 27+ weeks in September 2026. Grade A. https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/empsit_10022026.htm (taken from search summary; not opened).
- Conflict: a seasonally adjusted median of 30.9 weeks was listed for February 2026 in table A-12 by a search summary. It differs sharply from the 12.8 above; I did not resolve it. https://www.bls.gov/web/empsit/cpseea12.htm
- Gap penalty: randomized resume audit of 12,054 resumes to 3,040 postings found callbacks about 45% lower at 8 months of unemployment than at 1 month (about 7% to 4%), per the J-PAL summary; a different search summary said 25%, unconfirmed. Grade A. https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:qjecon:v:128:y:2013:i:3:p:1123-1167 and https://povertyactionlab.org/media/file-research-paper/dependence-duration-and-labor-market-conditions-evidence-united-states

## 4. The hidden job market: myth or evidence

- FACT: the "70-80% of jobs are never advertised" figure has no traceable primary source. Career-center pages say it is hard to trace and likely arose from anecdote and 1970s-80s career literature. Grade B/D. https://career.ucf.edu/blog/2024/08/12/is-it-true-that-80-of-job-openings-are-not-advertised/
- FACT: referrals are a real channel, about 17% of hires and about 1% of applications in one vendor dataset (section 1b). That is a measured "less visible" share, not 80%.
- FACT: weak-tie contacts causally increase job mobility (section 1c, grade A).
- ESTIMATE: the unadvertised share is not measured by any source I found; it varies by job level and sector and is probably well below 80% for typical hires. This is inference, not evidence.

## 5. ATS usage and resume parsing: facts vs myths

- Usage: Jobscan reports 97.8% to 98.4% of Fortune 500 companies use an ATS (figure varies by edition; vendor). Grade D. https://www.jobscan.co/blog/8-things-you-need-to-know-about-applicant-tracking-systems/
- Auto-rejection myth: Huntr (analysis of 1.7M applications plus recruiter interviews) says no major ATS auto-rejects resumes, but knockout questions can disqualify. Grade D. https://huntr.co/blog/how-applicant-tracking-systems-work
- Recruiter survey claims (92% or 83% say ATS does not auto-reject) cannot be traced to an original study; the figures conflict. Grade D, unconfirmed. https://careery.pro/blog/ats-friendly-resume-guide
- Jobscan itself says an ATS acts as a search and ranking tool. https://www.jobscan.co/blog/spot-ats/
- Practical reading (ESTIMATE): the real risk is knockout questions, ranking and keyword search by recruiters, and parse errors, not silent automatic rejection. Not independently tested.
- AI screening is spreading: Greenhouse says 25% of hiring managers use AI to screen. Grade C. https://www.unleash.ai/artificial-intelligence/greenhouse-25-of-hiring-managers-use-ai-to-screen-applicants-yet-8-are-unsure-what-the-ai-prioritizes/

## 6. AI in applications and employer reaction

All from the Greenhouse AI in Hiring report, Nov 2025 (vendor survey, grade C).
- About three in four job seekers use AI; 41% of US job seekers admit prompt injection (hidden text). Source: https://www.entrepreneur.com/business-news/ai-is-making-it-easier-to-apply-for-and-harder-to-find-a-job-than-ever
- 91% of recruiters say they spotted candidate deception; 34% spend up to half their week filtering spam. Source: https://www.greenhouse.com/newsroom/an-ai-trust-crisis-70-of-hiring-managers-trust-ai-to-make-faster-and-better-hiring-decisions-only-8-of-job-seekers-call-it-fair
- Fortune's coverage gives 65% of US hiring managers having caught deceptive AI use; metric differs. https://fortune.com/2025/11/18/hiring-job-seekers-recruiters-talent-acquisition-ai-doom-loop-application-technology/
- 61% of US employers use AI-detection software; 46% of job seekers say trust fell; only 8% think AI screening is fair. Source: https://www.cpapracticeadvisor.com/2025/11/24/survey-70-of-hiring-managers-say-they-trust-ai-to-make-faster-better-hiring-decisions/173845/
- Ashby: applications interviewed per hire rose from 6.3 (2021) to 13.3 (Q2 2026), EMEA; secondary. https://briefglance.com/articles/the-emea-paradox-how-recruiters-tamed-the-application-tsunami
- ESTIMATE: mass AI-generated applications raise recruiter noise, which plausibly lowers the value of generic applications and raises the value of verified signals (referrals, specific evidence). Inference, no direct test found.

## 7. Salary negotiation

- Cullen, Perez-Truglia, Pakzad-Hurson study of about 3,858 tech job seekers recruited via levels.fyi, May 2023 to Feb 2025: about 42% countered, about 85% of those got some of what they asked, and counter-offers raised compensation about 12.45% (about $27,000 a year in their sample). Grade B (working paper via UCLA brief), tech-sector sample. https://anderson-review.ucla.edu/most-job-seekers-skip-negotiation-and-pay-a-high-price/
- Pew (secondary, unverified): about 66% of workers who negotiated starting pay said they got what they asked for. Grade B/D. https://www.gobankingrates.com/money/jobs/gen-z-more-likely-negotiate-starting-salary-heres-why/
- Resume Genius 2025 (1,000 US workers): 78% of negotiators got a better offer; 14% got what they wanted. Grade C/D. https://resumegenius.com/blog/job-hunting/salary-expectations
- The three figures define success differently; do not compare directly.

## 8. Tailoring, cover letters, follow-ups

- Tailoring: no randomized study isolating tailoring found. Vendor claims: Jobscan 63% more callbacks (methods unverified); Wellfound 2.1x interview conversion (observational). Grade D. https://wellfound.com/blog/how-to-double-your-interview-rate-what-500-000-job-applications-revealed and https://huntr.co/research/job-search-trends-q1-2026
- Resume content does change callbacks in randomized audits: a Peru audit found listing a scholarship raised callbacks by 20%; a UK audit (N = 9,022) found listing years worked instead of dates raised callbacks for workers with gaps. Grade A/B. https://docs.iza.org/dp17004.pdf and https://ore.exeter.ac.uk/rest/bitstreams/189312/retrieve (identification from search summary; paper-to-claim mapping not verified)
- Cover letters: no randomized evidence found. NOT FOUND.
- Thank-you notes: Accountemps survey of 300+ HR managers, nearly 80% say they factor into the decision; only 24% had received any (51% in 2007). Self-reported, a different write-up says 51% (conflict). Grade B/D. https://prnewswire.com/news-releases/thank-you-notes-can-tip-scale-in-job-candidates-favor-yet-few-write-them-300559082.html
- Application follow-ups: no causal study found. NOT FOUND.

## 9. Ghost jobs

- Greenhouse: 18-22% of postings were ghost jobs (Q2 2024 data); about 70% of its employer customers posted at least one. Grade C. https://www.entrepreneur.com/business-news/ghost-job-listings-on-the-rise-how-to-spot-avoid-experts/485494
- Higher: ResumeUp.AI estimates 27.4% of LinkedIn US postings; LiveCareer survey says 45% of HR professionals post ghost jobs regularly. Grade D. https://www.fox6now.com/news/ghost-jobs-greenhouse-analysis
- CRS In Focus IF12977 (25 April 2025): no official statistics on ghost-job magnitude. Grade A on that point. https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/IF/PDF/IF12977.2.pdf
- BLS reportedly said JOLTS is not based on online postings and so is unaffected by ghost jobs (secondary, unconfirmed). https://fortune.com/2026/06/18/exclusive-arizona-senator-gallego-ghost-jobs-warp-labor-data/

## 10. Career change from operations into business roles without technical credentials

Weakest-evidenced section. I found no study tracking operations-to-business-role transitions.
- Skills-based hiring is spreading in stated policy (NACE: 70%) but actual practice is thin: one cited estimate says it caused about 1 in 700 hires in 2023. Grade D. https://www.shrm.org/topics-tools/news/talent-acquisition/skills-first-hiring-advances-career-mobility-paper-ceiling
- Opportunity@Work: workers skilled through alternative routes lost access to 7.5 million jobs 2000-2020; advocacy source. Grade C/D. https://workingnation.com/the-state-of-the-paper-ceiling/
- Pathway reasoning (ESTIMATE, not evidence): internal moves, referrals from cross-functional colleagues, and agency or temp routes have better measured conversion than cold inbound (section 1). The Ashby numbers show the channel advantage, not that it holds for career changers.
- Not located: Burning Glass Institute / Harvard Business School skills-based hiring report; I could not verify its findings.

Notes on the table:
- Levers 24 and 25 sit at the bottom because evidence is absent, not because effects are shown to be small. The URLs on rows 24 and 25 are context pages, not evidence for the effect; this is a weakness.
- Levers 2-4 are selection-confounded; the referred candidate is already pre-screened. Do not read them as the uplift from asking for a referral.
- Only levers 1, 5, 6 and 22 rest on a randomized design (1 on survey plus experiment data). Everything else is observational, vendor-reported or survey-reported.
